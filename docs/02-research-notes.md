# Research Notes (gathered September 2026)

## Burden on families
- Parents of kids with eczema reported about 87 restless nights a year vs. 23 for other parents (PEDI-BURDEN study, Italy).
- AAP: 50–80% of children with eczema have sleep problems; average time to fall asleep is about 40 minutes.
- Families rank eczema (with psoriasis) second only to cerebral palsy in effect on quality of life (AAP).
- A review of 54 studies: eczema disrupts parents' careers, sleep, and emotions, and can strain the parent-child bond.
- A caregiver study is titled with a parent's words: "I'm tired, overwhelmed, and feel like I'm failing as a mother."

## Money
- NEA survey: children with eczema had median out-of-pocket costs of $860/year; about half of families spent $1,000+.
- Parents spend more than adults with eczema on moisturizers, bathing products, childcare, cleaning products, and clothing/bedding.

## Insurance
- NEA data (Feb 2026): nearly 40% of patients/caregivers hit insurance barriers; 15% never started a prescribed medication due to coverage.
- Step therapy forces kids through a chain of treatments (steroids, then cheaper non-steroid creams, then pricier ones) before biologics are covered. Documented treatment history is what gets approvals and appeals through.

## Food
- Reactions can be delayed 12–24 hours (especially through breast milk). Testing one food takes 2–4 weeks.
- Histamine: fresh food is lower; leftovers, aged, and fermented foods are higher.
- Safety: long, unsupervised elimination diets risk nutritional deficiencies and may contribute to developing true food allergies (Canadian Family Physician review; Solid Starts). Dietitians warn over-restriction increases mealtime anxiety.
- TSW: IgE levels to many foods can run high after stopping steroids without meaning a true allergy.
- Design takeaway: help the safe food list GROW (reintroduction, variety, nutrient coverage).

## TSW and steroid fear
- 21–77% of parents of pediatric eczema patients report steroid phobia (Pediatric Dermatology).
- TSW is real to many families and contested by many dermatologists. Taking a side costs us one group. The app stays neutral: it runs the family's chosen plan.

## Why health apps fail
- A review of 500,000+ users: a median 70% quit within 100 days. Reasons: technical issues, privacy, poor UX, content, time/cost, changing needs.
- Huckleberry (5M+ families) wins by: free logging, one-hand use, multi-caregiver sync, night mode, and charging for ANSWERS (SweetSpot nap predictions), plus logging by text/voice/photo. Plus ~$5.74/mo, Premium ~$9.99/mo (billed yearly).
- Lesson: log free, pay for answers.

## Competitors
- EczemaWise (NEA): shutting down Dec 18, 2026; new sign-ups closed; only tracked one person.
- 200+ small eczema tracker apps on iOS; none dominant.
- Fig: grocery barcode scanning for 2,800+ diets incl. low histamine, family profiles, recipes. Don't compete on scanning.
- SuperCook: recipes from what you have; very limited dietary restriction filters.
- Histamine list apps: static food references, no link to the child's skin.

## Affiliate economics
- The Eczema Company: at least 6% commission, ~$75 average order, cookie as short as 7 days (about $4.50/order).
- EczemaHoney: 12%.
- Amazon Associates for general products.
- Bigger-ticket items (air purifiers, HEPA vacuums, dehumidifiers) earn more per sale.
- Affiliate is the second revenue stream; subscriptions are the main one.

## Real care plan example (natural protocol, anonymized)
Structure of a real practitioner plan for a 5-year-old, useful for designing care plan import:
- Areas of focus (rotation diet, gut support, possible further testing at 4–6 weeks)
- Fundamentals: air (daily airing out, HEPA vacuum 2–3x/week, humidity), bowel movements, hydration (amount left blank), movement, sleep
- Supplements table: name, brand, dose, frequency, directions, duration, blank start/stop dates; rules like "add one at a time, 3–5 days apart," "start with a drop," "start probiotic 2 weeks after antimicrobial," "rotate antimicrobial after 3 weeks"
- Diet: avoid list, 4-day rotation by food family, 40–50 plants/week goal, 3–6 months
- Topical routine: 3 steps, 3–4x/day, patch testing on forearm
- Baths: rotate, don't combine
- Tracking format: daily journal of changes, rash/itch, bowel movements, sleep, mood
- Provider limits: 5 follow-up messages within 8 weeks, follow-up visit at 4–6 weeks
